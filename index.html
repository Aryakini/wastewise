Introduction

Every home, hostel and campus produces waste every single day. Peels, plastic wrappers, old notebooks, dead batteries - most of it ends up in the same bin and then in the same landfill.

Waste management is the process of collecting, sorting, treating and disposing of waste so that it does the least harm to people and the environment. The good news is that it starts with a small habit: putting each item in the right bin.

WasteWise is a simple guide that makes that habit easy. Look up an item, learn where it goes, and find out if it can be reused or recycled before it is thrown away.

Our mission

To make correct waste disposal simple enough that anyone can do it without thinking twice, and to show that most "waste" still has a second life.

  • ReduceBuy and use only what you need.
  • ReuseGive old things a new job before binning them.
  • RecycleSend clean, sorted material back into production.

Waste management: pros and cons

Good waste management has clear benefits, but it also comes with real challenges. Both are worth knowing.

Pros

  • Keeps streets, drains and water bodies cleaner.
  • Reduces diseases spread by rotting garbage, flies and rats.
  • Saves raw materials and energy through recycling.
  • Turns food and garden waste into compost.
  • Cuts the amount of waste sent to landfills.
  • Creates jobs in collection, sorting and recycling.

Cons

  • Collection, transport and treatment cost money.
  • Recycling plants need energy and can pollute if badly run.
  • Many people do not sort waste at home, so material gets contaminated.
  • Some waste, like mixed plastics, is hard to recycle.
  • Landfills and burning waste release harmful gases.
  • Cities often lack enough bins, trucks and processing sites.
